# Method decisions and curriculum differences

The assigned list is National Curriculum Chemistry IX-X (2006), Chapter 5: naphthalene, biphenyl, acetone, benzene and ethyl alcohol. This is the user's chosen set. The Punjab practical notebook's 5.4 page instead names toluene. The app keeps **benzene** and identifies that difference; it does not claim those two lists are identical.

The locally inspected Punjab Chemistry IX practical PDF is the official PCTB web-version notebook (printed pages 12-18). Its method uses a 250 cm3 beaker with approximately 100 cm3 water, a capillary with one sealed end, a 1 cm powder column, thread attachment, immersion without glass contact, continuous stirring, and **two separate runs whose readings are averaged**. For melting it records the start of melting; the app also retains the last-solid observation as an additional within-trial interval. That interval is not substituted for the two-trial mean.

The book's boiling endpoint is the start of a **continuous stream of bubbles** from the open end of an inverted capillary in a liquid-filled fusion tube. The app follows this assigned endpoint. More precise modern micro-methods can use liquid entry during cooling; that is a different endpoint and is not silently mixed into the book worksheet.

The boiling apparatus uses an electric water-bath heater. The book illustration contains a Bunsen burner, but acetone, ethanol and benzene are flammable. Sealing empty capillaries is a separate virtual glass-preparation operation before solvent is dispensed. Benzene remains a virtual-only school exercise; it is carcinogenic. Real handling requires the school's approved method and supervision.

Reference transitions are educational nominal values near atmospheric pressure: naphthalene about 80 C, biphenyl about 69 C, acetone about 56 C, benzene about 80 C, and ethanol about 78 C. The model has bath/sample/instrument lag and explicit technique metrics; it is not a thermodynamic property solver. The accelerated clock and timing score tolerance are app settings, not laboratory rules.
